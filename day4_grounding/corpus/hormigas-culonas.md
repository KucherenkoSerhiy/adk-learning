# Hormigas Culonas — Colombia's Big-Butt Ant Delicacy

"Hormigas culonas" translates literally to "big-bottomed ants." They are
queen leafcutter ants of the species *Atta laevigata*, harvested in
Colombia's Santander department, and they are the single most famous
example of a "big ant butt" being eaten anywhere in the world — which makes
it worth being precise about what they actually taste like.

These queens are notably large, reaching up to roughly 2.5 cm in length,
with a distended gaster that gives them their name. The tradition dates back
to the indigenous Guane people, who regarded the egg-heavy females as
symbols of fertility. Harvest is highly seasonal — typically during the
rainy period between March and June, when queens emerge from their nests for
their nuptial flight — which is part of why they remain a prized, relatively
expensive delicacy rather than an everyday food.

Preparation is simple: fried or roasted with salt, sold in small bags at
markets and corner stores in Santander, with more upscale versions served in
restaurants. The taste, by every account, is **not sour**. It's consistently
described as nutty, closer to a blend of peanuts and bacon, with a crackling
texture on the first bite. Despite the name directly referencing the size of
their rear end, hormigas culonas are a flavor and texture delicacy, not a
sour one — the opposite pairing from what the "big butt = sour" assumption
would predict.
