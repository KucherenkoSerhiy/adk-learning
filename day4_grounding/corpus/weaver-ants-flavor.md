# Weaver Ants Don't Follow the Sour-Ant Rule

Weaver ants (genus *Oecophylla*, found across tropical Asia, Africa, and
Australia) are frequently eaten across their range — including in Thai and
Lao cuisine, most famously in dishes like ant egg soup — and are often
lumped in with "sour ants" in casual conversation. That reputation doesn't
hold up well under closer inspection.

Chemical analysis of weaver ant flavor compounds (presented at an American
Chemical Society meeting in 2024) found their taste is dominated by
pyrazines and pyrroles, giving a profile described as nutty, sweet, and
caramel-like — closer to roasted nuts than to citrus. Researchers also
detected hay- and urine-like off-notes from amine compounds at higher
concentrations, which is a real part of the flavor profile, not just an
unpleasant description.

In other words: weaver ants are a flavor exception among commonly-eaten
ants. Formic acid is still present, but it is not the dominant note the way
it is in black ants, harvester ants, or wood ants. Any claim that "all
edible ants taste sour" breaks specifically on this species.
